What is a funding rate?

A funding rate is a small payment between traders who hold perpetual futures. When the rate is positive, traders with long positions pay traders with short positions. When it is negative, shorts pay longs. Payments happen on a set schedule, for example every hour or every 8 hours depending on the exchange; the Paid every column shows the schedule of each market. The annualized rate spreads that periodic payment over a full year so you can compare venues at a glance.

How a carry trade works

A funding carry trade tries to collect funding while staying price-neutral. You short the perpetual where funding is high, so you receive payments, and hedge the price move by holding spot or going long on another venue where funding is lower. The main risks are funding flipping against you, trading fees eating the return, liquidation if prices move sharply, and venue or smart-contract failure where your funds are held.
